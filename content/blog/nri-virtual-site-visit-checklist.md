Buying an apartment from overseas is not inherently riskier than buying it in person — if you do it systematically. The mistake most NRI buyers make is not the distance. It is the absence of a structured process for what to look for, what to ask, and what documentation to verify before committing.

Here is a complete checklist built for buyers who cannot be on the ground.

## Before the Virtual Visit: Documentation Verification

Do this before you look at a single photograph or attend a video call.

**Developer verification:**
- Search the developer's name on TSRERA portal (tsrera.telangana.gov.in) — check their registered projects and compliance status
- Verify previous projects: were they delivered on time? Search "[developer name] possession delay" and "[developer name] reviews" independently
- Check if the company is registered with the relevant authorities — MCA21 (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) allows you to check company registration status

**Project verification:**
- Request the project's RERA registration number (if registered) and verify it independently on TSRERA
- Request the approved building plan (sanctioned plan from HMDA or GHMC)
- Request the land title documents or a title clearance certificate from the developer's lawyer
- Verify the developer has clear title to the land — this is the most fundamental check

**Developer's bank:**
- Ask which banks have approved the project for home loans. Bank approval requires their own due diligence on the developer and the project. Multiple major bank approvals (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis) are a positive signal.

## The Virtual Site Visit: What to Request

When you attend a virtual walkthrough — whether via WhatsApp video, Zoom, or pre-recorded video — ask for these specific views in this order.

**Site and location:**
- Live video from the boundary of the site, walking the perimeter if possible
- The surrounding roads and landmarks visible from the site
- The immediate neighbourhood — roads, buildings, walkability

**Building and structure:**
- Current construction status — foundation, structure, ongoing work
- The quality of concrete and construction visible on the structure
- Common area progress — lobby, corridors, lift shafts

**Showflat or sample flat:**
- Walk every room, not just the living area
- Check ceiling height (should be visibly generous — typically 10 feet or above in premium projects)
- Check balcony depth — walk to the edge, show that a chair fits comfortably
- Check bathroom dimensions — can the bathroom be navigated without squeezing?
- Check the kitchen — is the workflow logical? Is there adequate counter space?
- Open every door and window — check that they operate smoothly and seal properly

**Views from the floor:**
- If access to the relevant floor is possible, record the view from the unit's orientation
- Show what is in front of the apartment from each window direction
- Show what the adjacent towers or buildings look like from that perspective

## Documentation Checklist Post-Visit

After the virtual walkthrough, obtain these documents in writing:

- Floor plan with carpet area dimensions and compass orientation marked
- Site plan showing all towers and their relative positions
- Payment schedule with milestone definitions
- Draft Sale Agreement (before signing the final one)
- Maintenance charge estimate and what it covers
- Possession date commitment in writing
- Developer's RERA registration number and project details

## The Questions Only NRI Buyers Need to Ask

**Bank account for payments:** You will need an NRE (Non-Resident External) or NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account for property payments. Confirm which type is appropriate for your transaction structure with a CA familiar with FEMA rules.

**Power of attorney:** If you cannot be present for registration, you will need a Power of Attorney notarised in the country you are in and apostilled (for countries in the Hague Convention) or attested at the Indian consulate. Ask the developer's team how they have facilitated this for previous NRI buyers.

**Home loan eligibility:** NRI home loans are available from most major Indian banks. Interest rates and eligibility criteria differ slightly from resident loans. Get a pre-approval in principle before committing to a payment schedule.

**Repatriation of funds:** If the property is ever sold, the proceeds go into an NRO account and are subject to TDS and repatriation rules under FEMA. Understand this before committing, particularly if you intend to eventually repatriate the sale proceeds.

**Registration:** Property registration in India requires physical presence or a registered Power of Attorney holder. The developer's team should have a clear process for NRI registration — if they do not, it is a signal that they have not handled NRI transactions before.

## The Red Flags

Walk away from a project if, during the remote evaluation:

- The developer cannot provide TSRERA registration details or gives evasive answers about registration status
- They are reluctant to provide the draft sale agreement before you pay a booking advance
- They cannot name which banks have approved the project for home loans
- They pressure a quick decision without allowing documentation review time
- The virtual visit shows construction that appears to lag significantly behind claimed timelines

## The Genuine Advantage of a Careful Remote Evaluation

An NRI buyer who does thorough remote due diligence often enters a transaction better informed than a local buyer who visits twice and signs based on the feeling of the showflat. Distance, paradoxically, can enforce discipline.

The checklist is the equaliser. Use it.
