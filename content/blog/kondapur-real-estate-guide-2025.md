Kondapur has been Hyderabad's worst-kept secret for a decade. IT professionals already knew it — close enough to Financial District and Hitech City, far enough from the chaos of central Hyderabad, and served by schools, hospitals, and retail that most neighbourhoods spend a decade waiting for.

What's changed in 2025 is the supply side. High-rise residential projects are now entering a neighbourhood that has fully matured — and that changes the calculation for buyers.

## Why Kondapur Works for the IT Professional

The Financial District is roughly 15 minutes by car. Hitech City, 25 minutes. Gachibowli sits in between. If you work at one of the large IT campuses in this corridor, Kondapur places you within a manageable commute without forcing you to live on top of the work district.

That matters because living close to the Financial District itself is expensive and, frankly, less liveable — the neighbourhood is still developing residential infrastructure. Kondapur, by contrast, has:

- **CHIREC International School** — one of the most sought-after K-12 schools in the city, 8 minutes away
- **Apollo Hospital** — 10 minutes
- **AMB Cinemas and Mall** — 5 minutes
- **ISB Hyderabad** — 12 minutes
- **ORR access** — 10 minutes, which means reasonable connectivity to the airport and across the city

When you're evaluating a home, this kind of existing infrastructure is worth more than a promise of "upcoming development."

## What Makes a High-Rise Worth Buying in Kondapur

Not all high-rise projects are equal. In a fully developed neighbourhood like Kondapur, you're not buying based on future area potential — you're buying based on the project itself. That changes what you should scrutinise.

**Lifts per floor.** This is the single most underrated specification in high-rise residential. A 52-floor tower with 2 lifts and 8 homes per floor is a very different living experience from a tower with 8 lifts. Morning peak hours will tell you the difference in 6 months. Ask specifically: how many lifts serve how many apartments?

**Where the amenities are.** A gym at the rooftop sounds impressive until you realise it adds 5 minutes to every gym session. Stilt-level amenities — at the base of the building, accessible without a second elevator ride — are practically superior. This is worth asking specifically about.

**Floor planning efficiency.** Developers frequently advertise carpet area as if it's the only thing that matters. What matters equally is how that area is used. A 1,900 sq ft apartment with a long wasteful corridor and small bedrooms is less liveable than a 1,750 sq ft apartment with efficient circulation and well-proportioned rooms. Ask for the floor plan before the area measurement.

**Vastu compliance.** In Hyderabad's real estate market, vastu-aligned floor plans are not a niche preference — they affect resale value significantly. Buyers should ask whether vastu has been designed into the tower orientation and individual floor plans, or just added as a marketing label.

## The 3 BHK Premium: Why It Makes Sense in 2025

Dual-income tech households in Hyderabad have broadly moved past the "buy the smallest we can afford" calculation. With both partners working, a home office is now a real requirement. Guest accommodation for parents is a real requirement. A proper master bedroom suite — not a master bedroom with a token window — is a real requirement.

The 3 BHK premium in Kondapur reflects this. Buyers in this market are increasingly looking at 1,800–2,500 sq ft as the sensible range: large enough to live in fully, not so large that maintenance becomes a burden.

At ₹1.6 Cr starting for a thoughtfully planned 3 BHK in a 52-floor high-rise in a fully developed Kondapur location, the value proposition is genuine — particularly when you factor in the infrastructure that's already in place and won't require a 5-year wait.

## What to Watch Out For

A few patterns worth avoiding when evaluating high-rise projects in this market:

- **Projects selling on "views" without addressing planning.** Views are a function of floor and orientation. Planning is a function of every floor. Don't let one override the other.
- **Overpromised possession timelines.** Mid-2030 for a project launching in mid-2025 is a realistic 5-year schedule. Promises of 3-year delivery on a project of this scale deserve scrutiny.
- **Amenity lists without square footage.** A list of 30 amenities can mean 30,000 sq ft or 90,000 sq ft of amenity space. Ask for the clubhouse area specifically.
- **Density without context.** 1,000+ homes across 5.5 acres and 3 towers is a specific density. The question isn't whether that's high — it's whether the planning accounts for it in lifts, circulation, and everyday usability.

The Kondapur market in 2025 rewards buyers who do their homework. The neighbourhood itself is no longer the variable — the project is.
