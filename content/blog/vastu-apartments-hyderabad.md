Vastu compliance is listed as a feature in roughly two-thirds of premium residential projects in Hyderabad. It's also one of the most frequently misunderstood claims in real estate marketing.

In a standalone home, vastu is relatively straightforward to implement — the plot, the entry, the kitchen, and the master bedroom can all be oriented according to vastu principles from the ground up. In a high-rise with hundreds of apartments stacked on top of each other, the situation is more complex. Not impossible, but meaningfully different.

Here's what vastu actually means at high-rise scale, what genuine compliance looks like, and what to ask before buying.

## Why High-Rise Vastu Is Different

In a standalone home, you can orient every room to cardinal directions. In a high-rise, most apartments share walls, floors, and ceilings with other apartments. The absolute orientation of each room is a function of where the apartment sits in the tower — and that's a planning decision made at the design stage.

This means vastu compliance in a high-rise cannot be an afterthought. It has to be baked into:

1. **Tower orientation** — which direction the building faces relative to north
2. **Floor plan layout** — where rooms are placed within each apartment
3. **Entrance placement** — the direction a resident faces when entering the apartment
4. **Utility placement** — where the kitchen, bathrooms, and storage are positioned

A developer who "added vastu" late in the design process can only do so at a cosmetic level. A developer who incorporated vastu from the beginning will have different tower orientations, different room arrangements, and different entrance placements — all of which are visible in the architectural drawings.

## The Five Vastu Questions to Ask

When evaluating a high-rise for vastu compliance, ask these five questions specifically:

**1. What is the primary facing direction of the towers?**
Northeast and north-facing towers are generally favoured in vastu. East-facing is also considered positive. South-facing towers are more challenging to work with and require more careful internal planning.

**2. Where is the main apartment entrance relative to compass directions?**
Most vastu traditions favour entrances in the north, northeast, or east. An entrance that faces southwest is generally unfavourable. In a high-rise, this is partially a function of which side of the building the apartment is on — which is why tower orientation matters.

**3. Where is the kitchen placed?**
The southeast corner (the fire zone in vastu) is the traditional placement for kitchens. Kitchens in the northeast or north are considered less favourable. Ask specifically where the kitchen is in the floor plan and which direction it faces.

**4. Is the master bedroom in the southwest?**
The southwest is associated with earth energy and stability in vastu — which is why it's traditionally preferred for the master bedroom. Bedrooms in the northeast are generally avoided. This is visible in the floor plan.

**5. Is the central space of the apartment open?**
The Brahmasthan — the central zone of any structure — is ideally kept open and unobstructed in vastu. In apartments, this typically means the living room or a well-lit central space rather than a corridor or storage room.

## What Genuine Vastu Planning Looks Like

A developer who has genuinely incorporated vastu into a high-rise project can answer all five questions above specifically — not in generalised marketing language, but with reference to the actual drawings.

The answers will often involve trade-offs. In a large tower with many different apartment types, not every unit can achieve perfect vastu compliance in every dimension. What genuine vastu planning produces is a considered set of choices that prioritise the most important factors — entrance direction, master bedroom placement, kitchen orientation — and document them clearly.

What it doesn't produce is a stamp on a brochure that says "vastu compliant" without any supporting specifics.

## Vastu and Resale Value in Hyderabad

This deserves a direct statement: in Hyderabad's premium residential market, vastu compliance has a material effect on resale value and rental demand. Buyers who wouldn't personally consider vastu as a priority still prefer vastu-compliant apartments when reselling, because they know a larger pool of buyers will consider it.

This is particularly true in the ₹1.5–3 Cr range, where buyers tend to be more personally invested in the vastu characteristics of their home, and where the pool of buyers is large enough that vastu-non-compliant apartments see longer time-on-market.

If you're buying primarily as an investment, vastu compliance is worth considering even if it's not personally important to you.

## The Bottom Line

Vastu-compliant high-rise living is achievable — but only when planned from the beginning, not added as a label at the end. The questions to ask are specific: tower orientation, entrance direction, kitchen placement, master bedroom location, and central space configuration.

Developers who have genuinely done the work will answer these questions specifically. Those who haven't will be vague. The specificity of the answer is itself a signal.
